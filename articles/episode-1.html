Episode 1 / Take 1 / Here Comes Spring

Greetings to the reader of this article.

In several parts, I wanted to share with you something of my approach to composition. Now it is springtime.

Part One

When I first began playing an instrument, I always had one question: how does a composer, or the creator of a sound-based work, actually create a piece? Where does one begin preparing? With the instrument? The inner voice? The sounds outside? Exploring nature?...

Then I realized that the further I went, the farther I drifted from the heart of the matter. During the golden years of university, our dear teacher Sahakian was teaching harmony, and I took it upon myself to study the subject more fully, specifically becoming his student and accepting all of his strict demands.

Eventually, I arrived at an interesting approach, though it remains somewhat mysterious even now. Once all the semesters were over, after all those strict rules of harmony and counterpoint—which there is no room to explain in this article—I understood that one could quite easily reject all those things and the Church’s rigid rules: first a framework, then creating within it under considerable stress, and finally becoming free of it and creating more without that stress.

That brought me about ten percent of the way toward an answer. Those golden years became a path toward further learning on my own—something I share with you on this website and on Instagram.

For example, my cover of the music from the film Schindler’s List began with careful listening to the violin line, which I then transformed for the setar. But there is no fixed formula for this. It takes careful thought about where on the instrument, in what position, and with what kind of stroke to play, so the music conveys the film’s melancholic mood and black-and-white atmosphere.

In some scenes of devastation, certain notes are not played fully, and their frequencies fade quickly—something that may even sound unpleasant to some lovers of classical music.

That is as far as we will go for now, until we return for Episode 2: Summer, Creating My Own Pieces—“Karkas,” “Degareh-ye Nagah,” and...

Thank you for reading.
